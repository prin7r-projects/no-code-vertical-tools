# 05 — Audience Profile

## Buyer-side ICP

**Owner-operator or founder of a 2-12-person business in a trade vertical that has one persistent operational workflow that hurts.**

Filter:
- Annual revenue $250k - $5M
- 2-12 employees (not a solo, not enterprise)
- One vertical: HVAC / dental / restaurant / salon-spa / agency-ops / SaaS-ops / e-commerce / roofing / law / clinic
- Already pays for at least 2 SaaS tools (so they're not "anti-software")
- Already takes credit cards or stablecoin (so the install fits their existing money flow)
- Has named the painful workflow themselves in the last 90 days

Anti-filter (we *don't* sell to):
- Enterprise IT shoppers expecting an RFP
- Pre-revenue founders looking for a "platform"
- Buyers whose painful workflow is "all of finance" or "all of sales" — too vague
- Anyone who frames the call as "let's brainstorm what we could build"

## Persona — Patricia (P1, primary)

**Patricia, 47** — owner-operator of Bryant Heating & Cooling, 6 employees + 4 trucks, Charlotte NC.

- **Time on the truck**: 3 days a week. Friday is admin day.
- **Education**: Trade school + 14 years on her father's HVAC business before she took it over.
- **Software stack today**: ServiceTitan ($375/mo, hates it), QuickBooks Online ($45/mo), Google Workspace ($18/mo), Squarespace ($23/mo for the marketing site).
- **Goal this quarter**: cut the time from on-site walk to a signed quote to under 24 hours, ideally same-day.
- **Frustrations with current alternatives**: bid on two no-code agency proposals in March (ChartHQ and a Charlotte freelancer); both quoted >$10k and 5+ weeks. She tested a "form-to-PDF" Bubble template from the marketplace and bounced after a weekend.
- **Channels she lives in**:
  - Facebook group: *Charlotte HVAC Contractors* (1.4k members)
  - Reddit: occasional `/r/HVAC` and `/r/smallbusiness`
  - Newsletter: ACCA + Pearl HVAC News
  - Podcast on the truck: *HVAC Hindsight*
  - Word-of-mouth from her county trade association meetings
- **Buys software when**: a peer in the FB group says "this finally fixed it" with a screenshot and a link.
- **Refunds when**: it took >2 hours to install or it tried to teach her something instead of solving the one thing.
- **Trust signals she looks for**: a refund policy in plain English, a price visible without a call, someone she knows uses it, the brand looks like *one thing for one trade* (not "no-code platform for everyone").

**Quote we expect from her after install**:
> "I sent five quotes this week. Three signed by Tuesday. I haven't been able to do that since I took over."

## Persona — Yuki (P1b, primary)

**Yuki, 39** — practice manager at a 2-dentist clinic in Vancouver BC, 8 staff (incl. dentists, hygienists, front desk, lab tech).

- **Authority**: she has the corporate card and the OK to spend up to $250/mo on software without a partners' meeting.
- **Software stack today**: Dentrix (practice management), Twilio (SMS), Stripe (payments), Google Workspace, a hand-rolled Sheets dashboard.
- **Goal this quarter**: cut no-shows by 20%. Last quarter the clinic lost ~$12k in opportunity cost from no-shows.
- **Frustrations with current alternatives**: Dentrix has a built-in reminder feature but it sends to *everyone* and it's annoying. The custom Twilio + Sheets dashboard works for the front desk but doesn't actually predict no-shows.
- **Channels she lives in**:
  - Local dental association quarterly call
  - LinkedIn (very lightly — sees a dental-tech newsletter)
  - Direct vendor demo emails (she opens the ones with screenshots, ignores the rest)
- **Buys software when**: the demo is a 90-second screen recording and the pricing is on the page.
- **Refunds when**: it takes more than two weeks to break even on the no-show savings.

## Anti-personas

**Anti-A — "Build me a CRM" guy.** Says: "We're a 50-person sales team and we need a custom CRM that integrates with our 11 systems." Wrong buyer; we don't sell custom CRMs. Refer him to a no-code agency.

**Anti-B — "What's possible?" tinkerer.** Says: "I'm not sure exactly what I need but I love no-code. Tell me what's possible." Wrong buyer; he doesn't have the painful workflow named yet. He'll churn in week 2.

**Anti-C — "We'll figure it out on the call" enterprise procurement.** Wants a 90-min discovery call, then an MSA, then a security questionnaire, then a 6-week pilot, then a contract. Wrong buyer; a $39/mo SKU can't carry the procurement overhead.

**Anti-D — "I'll just learn no-code over the weekend" DIY founder.** Wrong buyer; he'll cancel in 5 days and rebuild it in Bubble himself. Fine — let him.

## Vertical breakdown — wave 2 catalog focus

| Vertical | Example sub-segment | Painful workflow we sell to |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC | 2-12 truck residential | Quote PDF turnaround; site walk → signed in 24h |
| Dental | 2-3 dentist private practice | No-show prediction + SMS reminder |
| Restaurant | 1-3 location independent FOH | Table-turn optimization on Friday/Saturday peak |
| Salon / spa | 3-15 chair, 2-12 stylist | 6-week rebook reminder with stylist match |
| Agency-ops | 3-15 person creative / digital | Invoice chaser + drafted escalation |
| SaaS-ops | 5-30 person B2B SaaS | Churn signal alert + named CS owner |
| E-commerce | $250k-$5M Shopify | WhatsApp abandoned-cart 9-min nudge |
| Roofing (q3) | 2-8 crew residential | Scope-of-work email from photo tags |

We pick the *one* most reliably painful workflow per vertical and ship that as the SKU. We don't try to sell three tools to the same buyer in month one.

## Buying motion

- **Trigger**: the buyer hits the painful workflow themselves, in the last 30-90 days, ideally because of a missed revenue or a customer complaint.
- **Discovery channel**: peer mention (FB group, association call, Reddit thread, LinkedIn post). NOT cold outbound.
- **Decision time**: 3-15 days from first visit to first install.
- **Decision unit**: the owner-operator herself (P1) or the practice manager with the corporate card (P1b). NOT a committee.
- **Time to first value**: <72 hours (most installs are scheduled within 24 hours of payment, install takes <4 hours).
- **Refund risk window**: days 0-30. After day 30, churn risk is dominated by external changes (no-code platform breakage, vendor pricing change), not buyer-side dissatisfaction.
